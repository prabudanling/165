## 1. MASTER IDEA

165.web.id must not be treated as a normal religious website or as an extension of social media.

It should be designed as a **digital knowledge, heritage, education, documentation, preservation and community infrastructure** for TQN Qodiriyah wa Naqsyabandiyah, with a global audience and a rigorous editorial provenance system.

### Core mission
1. Preserve: manuscripts, books, photographs, audio, video, oral histories, biographies, places and institutional records.
2. Explain: history, concepts, terminology, figures and intellectual genealogy in accessible language.
3. Verify: distinguish documented history, institutional/traditional narratives, and 165 interpretation.
4. Connect: researchers, students, communities, institutions, archives and contributors.
5. Serve: provide useful educational and community resources without pretending that a website replaces the living spiritual teacher, authorized institution, or legitimate spiritual transmission.

### Proposed positioning
> **165.web.id — The Global Knowledge, Heritage & Digital Preservation Platform for TQN Qodiriyah wa Naqsyabandiyah.**

Possible Indonesian positioning:
> **165.web.id — Pusat Pengetahuan, Warisan, Dokumentasi dan Pelestarian Digital TQN Qodiriyah wa Naqsyabandiyah untuk dunia.**

## 2. THE 165 EDITORIAL CONSTITUTION

Every important page should follow five rules.

### Rule A — Provenance before eloquence
Every historical claim should have a source, author/institution, date, and—where relevant—page/manuscript reference.

### Rule B — Three truth layers
Label information as:
- **Documented History** — supported by traceable historical/academic/archival sources.
- **TQN/Institutional Tradition** — a lineage, recollection, hagiographic account, or institutional narrative presented as such.
- **165 Interpretation** — a contemporary educational or strategic synthesis created by 165.

### Rule C — No invented silsilah
165 should never manufacture, “complete,” or silently reconcile a lineage that is contested or insufficiently documented. Publish competing versions with attribution and source notes when needed.

### Rule D — Spiritual authority is not created by a website
Website membership, badges, subscriptions, or digital status must never be presented as equivalent to talqin, bai‘at, khirqah, ijazah, kemursyidan, or other forms of authorized spiritual transmission.

### Rule E — Preserve the difference between education and initiation
Educational content can be public. Restricted spiritual practices, confidential guidance, or materials that a particular TQN authority considers non-public should only be published with appropriate authorization.

## 3. HISTORY: FROM THE UPSTREAM TO THE DIGITAL PRESENT

### 3.1 The wider Sufi context
Tarekat should be introduced within the wider history of Islamic spirituality rather than appearing to begin abruptly with one organization. 165 should explain the relationship among **Islam — shari‘ah — tasawwuf — tariqah — adab — dzikr — suluk — ihsan**, while making clear that these terms have varied usages across periods and schools.

The website should avoid treating one later institutional form as identical to the entire history of Sufism.

### 3.2 Qadiriyya and Shaykh `Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani
`Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani (1077/78–1166) was a Hanbali scholar, preacher and Sufi shaykh associated with Baghdad; the Qadiri order took his name and developed historically after him. Encyclopaedia Iranica identifies him as the eponymous founder of the Qadiri order.  

**165 lesson:** authority is preserved through learning, adab, practice and transmission—not merely through branding.

### 3.3 Naqshbandiyya and Baha al-Din Naqshband
Baha al-Din Naqshband (1318–1389) became the eponym of the Naqshbandiyya, one of the major Sufi orders. Encyclopaedia Iranica notes the importance of disciplined adherence to shari‘ah and silent dhikr within the early Naqshbandi tradition.  

**165 lesson:** inwardness and disciplined conduct should not be separated from outward ethical life.

### 3.4 Ahmad Khatib Sambas and the TQN formation
Modern academic literature commonly identifies Shaykh Ahmad Khatib Sambas (1803–1875) as the Nusantara shaykh who formulated the Qadiriyah wa Naqsyabandiyah synthesis in the nineteenth century. Research based on manuscripts of *Fath al-‘Arifin* provides evidence that Ahmad Khatib had transmission links through both Qadiriyah and Naqshbandiyah.  

**165 lesson:** TQN carries an important Nusantara intellectual history; it should be documented as a serious historical and scholarly subject, not merely as folklore.

### 3.5 Transmission into Java
Research on the genealogy and spread of TQN in Java identifies major transmission lines through figures such as Shaykh Abdul Karim Banten, Shaykh Ahmad Thalhah Cirebon, and Shaykh Muhammad Hasbullah Madura, followed by several major centers in Java including Suryalaya, Rejoso, Mranggen and Pangentongan.  

**165 lesson:** the history of TQN is a network, not a single straight line.

### 3.6 Suryalaya and Abah Sepuh
The official history of Pondok Pesantren Suryalaya places the establishment of the pesantren at Godebag on 5 September 1905 and describes the role of Shaykh Tholhah bin Talabudin of Cirebon in supporting and authorizing Abah Sepuh within the TQN tradition. The exact biographical chronology should always be sourced because some details differ across institutional and scholarly materials.  

**165 lesson:** institutions survive when spiritual teaching becomes disciplined education, community service and stewardship of place.

### 3.7 Abah Anom
The Suryalaya record describes KH A. Shohibulwafa Tajul Arifin (Abah Anom) as born in 1915 and succeeding Abah Sepuh in 1956. Under his leadership, Suryalaya expanded education, community development and the Inabah rehabilitation initiative, alongside the continued dissemination of TQN. Academic literature also studies his educational and social roles.  

**165 lesson:** a spiritual tradition can generate institutions that respond to real social needs while remaining rooted in its ethical vocabulary.

### 3.8 From physical heritage to digital heritage
This is the point where **165 begins as a contemporary project**. 165 should not claim to be a historical continuation unless that institutional authority is formally established. Its role can be defined more modestly and powerfully: **to document, preserve, explain, connect and make discoverable the history and heritage of TQN for future generations.**

**165 lesson:** the digital age creates a new amanah—preventing loss, distortion, disappearance and fragmentation of knowledge.

Design toward WCAG 2.2 accessibility principles so the archive is usable by people with disabilities. WCAG is the W3C international standard for web accessibility.

The visual language should be **sacred, archival, scholarly, calm, contemporary and unmistakably Nusantara**.

Avoid:
- excessive ornament
- pseudo-Arabic visual clichés
- sensational miracle imagery
- claims such as “the only true…” without authoritative basis
- a social-feed appearance as the homepage

Prefer:
- deep editorial typography
- manuscript/document textures used sparingly
- historical maps and timelines
- documentary photography
- clear source labels
- bilingual content
- restrained motion
- fast search
- generous whitespace

## 12. ARCHIVE & PRESERVATION POLICY

165 should behave like a digital archive, not only a blog.

### Minimum preservation stack
- master files kept separately from web derivatives
- 3-2-1 backup strategy
- checksums / fixity checks for archival files
- versioned metadata
- rights records
- controlled vocabularies
- persistent identifiers
- periodic restoration tests
- documented ingest workflow

For images/manuscripts, store an archival master and separate web derivatives. For important media, retain the original acquisition file where rights allow.

### Archive ingest workflow
Receive → identify → rights check → scan/transfer → checksum → metadata → description → editorial review → publish derivative → preserve master → audit.

## 13. RESEARCH & CITATION POLICY

For every serious historical page:

**Primary sources:** manuscripts, archival documents, institutional records, contemporary testimony.  
**Secondary sources:** scholarly books and peer-reviewed research.  
**Tertiary sources:** encyclopedias, summaries and educational pages.

When sources disagree:
> “Version A — source …”
> “Version B — source …”
> “165 editorial note — evidence currently available …”

Do not silently select the most flattering version.

## 20. THE 165 QUALITY BAR

Every flagship page should pass six tests:

**Truth** — Are claims sourced?  
**Context** — Is historical context adequate?  
**Transmission** — Is lineage/authority distinguished from biography?
